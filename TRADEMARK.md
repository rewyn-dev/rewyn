# Trademark Policy

The Rewyn software is licensed under Apache-2.0, which gives you broad rights
to use, modify and redistribute the **code**. It does not give you rights to
the **name**. Those are different things, and this page explains the
difference so you do not have to guess.

The name "Rewyn", the Rewyn logo, and the domain rewyn.dev are marks of the
project maintainers.

## What you may do, without asking

- **Say what your software does.** "Built with Rewyn", "compatible with
  Rewyn", "a Rewyn plugin", "runs Rewyn under the hood" — all fine.
- **Write about Rewyn.** Articles, talks, courses, tutorials, books, reviews,
  comparisons. Critical ones too.
- **Redistribute the software** under Apache-2.0, keeping the name, as long as
  you do not imply we produced or endorsed your distribution.
- **Use the name in a package that extends Rewyn**, as a suffix:
  `rewyn-postgres`, `rewyn-langchain`. Not as a prefix that reads like an
  official component.
- **Use the name in the title of a non-commercial community project**, meetup
  or user group.

## What needs permission

- **Naming your product, company or service after Rewyn**, or using a
  confusingly similar name.
- **Using the name for a hosted or commercial service** built on Rewyn — for
  example running "Rewyn Cloud", "Rewyn Enterprise", or "Managed Rewyn" as a
  paid offering. The maintainers run a commercial service under this name; two
  of those cannot coexist without confusing the people paying for one of them.
- **Registering domains, social accounts or package names** that suggest they
  are official.
- **Using the logo in your own branding**, or modifying it.
- **Selling merchandise** carrying the name or logo.

## What you may never do

- Present a modified version as the official one.
- Suggest the project endorses you, your product or your opinions when it
  does not.
- Use the name in a way that suggests a security review, certification or
  audit that has not happened.

## Forks

Fork freely — that is the point of an open licence. If you publish a fork that
diverges in behaviour, give it its own name. You may say "a fork of Rewyn" to
explain where it came from; you may not call it Rewyn.

## Why this exists

Open source works because anyone can use the code. Trademarks work because a
name means one thing. An SDK whose name could be attached to any modified
build is a name users cannot rely on, and a security fix nobody can locate.
Keeping the name specific is what makes "install Rewyn" a safe instruction.

If this policy makes something you want to do unclear, ask before assuming
the worst — open a discussion. We would rather say yes with a condition than
have you guess.
